Add subtitles to your YouTube video

You don't need desktop software or YouTube's clunky editor to caption a video. Paste your link, play it here, and type each line as it's spoken. Mark when each caption starts and ends with a keystroke, watch it appear on the video, then download a clean .srt and upload it back in YouTube Studio.

Caption a video →🔒 Free · no signup · saved in your browser

How it works

  1. Paste your YouTube URL to load the video here.
  2. Press play. When a line starts, press S; when it ends, press E.
  3. Type the caption. It appears on the video instantly so timing is never a guess.
  4. Work through the video, then download your .srt.
  5. In YouTube Studio, open Subtitles, add a language, and upload the .srt.

Frequently asked questions

Does it transcribe the audio for me?

Not for YouTube links. YouTube and Vimeo block audio download, so auto-captions cannot read them, and you type each line yourself with keyboard shortcuts while the captions show on the video. Solo and Team plans add AI auto-captions for uploaded video files and for direct, Dropbox, Google Drive, and Frame.io links. Review any auto-generated captions before you publish them.

Will the subtitles stay in sync on YouTube?

Yes. You set each in and out point against the actual video, and the exported .srt carries those exact timecodes, so it lines up when you upload it.

Do I lose my work if I close the tab?

No. Your captions are saved in your browser on your device for each video, so you can close the tab and pick up where you left off. If you are signed in, the project is also saved to your SubCaptions account.

Is it really free?

Captioning videos up to 45 minutes, exporting .srt and .vtt files, and one translation per video are free. Solo and Team plans add full-length videos, unlimited translations, AI auto-captions, and video upload. See the pricing page for details.
